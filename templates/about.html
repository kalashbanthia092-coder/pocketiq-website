Why this exists

Two problems kept showing up, so I built something for each.

Nobody teaches this at the right time

School covers a lot of things you'll use eventually. It mostly skips the ones you'll use next week: whether to buy the thing, how to make ₹500 last the month, why the same phone costs so differently across two sites.

By the time most people learn it, they've already picked up habits that are annoying to undo. Starting earlier is easier than fixing it later.

The advice that does exist is unusable

It's either too basic to help ("save more!") or written in a language that assumes you already know what an expense ratio is. Neither one gets you closer to a decision.

MoBo goes the other way. It asks about your actual situation, uses numbers you recognise, and gives you something you can act on today.

How I've tried to build it

A few rules I keep coming back to.

🗣️

Plain language, always

If a word needs a glossary, it needs a better sentence. Every lesson uses examples you'd actually run into: recharges, snacks, subscriptions, a first job.

🎯

Built around you

Your roadmap comes from what you wrote about yourself, not a fixed syllabus. Two people describing different starting points get genuinely different paths.

🤝

Honest, not pushy

MoBo isn't selling anything and doesn't get a cut. Sometimes the right answer is "wait and save", and it'll say that.
